# Usability and accessibility

## Readability
- [ ] Text contrast is at least 4.5:1 (3:1 for large text), measured at the worst point of the background, including see-through cards on a gradient.
- [ ] Meaning is never carried by color alone (also an icon, a word or a shape).
- [ ] Large system text sizes do not clip, overlap or hide buttons.
- [ ] Long text, emoji and right-to-left text do not break layouts.

## Reaching and operating
- [ ] Tap targets are at least 44 points, with space between neighbours.
- [ ] Icon-only buttons have a label and role for the screen reader; reading order follows the screen.
- [ ] Small screens: nothing off-screen or unreachable behind the keyboard; safe areas respected.
- [ ] The main task works one-handed and without precise gestures.
- [ ] Motion follows the reduce-motion setting and nothing moves forever on a screen the person must read.

## States and flows
- [ ] Loading, empty, error and offline states exist and say what to do next.
- [ ] Messages are plain words, say what happened and offer the next step.
- [ ] Destructive actions are confirmed or can be undone.
- [ ] The main task can be done from a blank start in few taps; every screen has a way back.
- [ ] Permission requests come with context, and the app still works if they are refused.
- [ ] Text is consistent: one word for one thing across screens, buttons and messages.
- [ ] A required step the person has not done is visible: the screen points at what is missing (a highlight and words), and tapping a dimmed button explains why instead of doing nothing.
- [ ] Anything that scrolls sideways shows it does (a partly visible item, an edge fade or an arrow), or the choices fit without scrolling.
- [ ] The same kind of page looks the same in every part of the app: title position and size, spacing, and how a risky action (delete, sign out) is drawn. Compare the screens side by side.

## Needs a device
- [ ] Screen-reader pass (reading order, labels), haptics, keyboard behaviour and a real small phone are checked by hand.
